Their old system worked. It just had to be rebuilt for every new brand.

The client serves quick service restaurants, where order-taking has to be fast, accurate, and identical in quality across very different menus. Their existing custom-trained transformers delivered that — but each new brand or menu structure meant another round of adaptation work.

They asked whether a large language model could replace that per-brand machinery. Unidata Lab built the answer: a fine-tuned GPT-4o-mini hosted on Azure that reads real customer orders and extracts menu items, modifiers and quantities.

Knowing when not to trust the model

The interesting engineering is not the extraction — it is the doubt. The team implemented several methods for scoring confidence, both for the recognised order state and for each extracted entity.

When confidence drops below threshold, the system triggers a fallback to human staff instead of committing a guess to someone's order. A prompt-engineering framework sits around the model to keep interpretation stable across the wide range of ways customers actually speak.
